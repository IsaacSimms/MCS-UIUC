# Probabilistic Topic Models: A form of topic mining
## TLDR
Treating a single term as a topic is too limited in most situations. Topics can be represented as a word distribution (a unigram langauge model).
This fixes many of the main problems and have become a foundation for the rest of topic mining. 

## Problem framing
With one term topics there are some issues. We solve those issues by:
- Topic = {Multiple words} solves the lack of expressive power issue
- Adding weights to words solves the incompleteness in vocabulary coverage issue (inability to capture variations in vocab)
- Spliting an ambiguous word fixes the word ambiguity issue
In a one topic non probabilistic model one word can have one topic.
But, once you enter into a probabilistic model each topic is a full word distribution. 
The same word is allowed a non-zero probability in multiple distributions.

### Solution: The word distribution
*each topic is not a single term. It is a probability distribution over the whole vocab.*

- Think of 'k' topics. Theta_1 = sports, Theta_2 = Travel, Theta_k = Science
For any topic Theta_i. P(w | Theta_i) is the probability that word w is under that topic. 
- Multiple words get probability mass, so tghe topic can be complex
- The probabilities end up being the weights
- Different distributions can place mass on different senses of the same word. 
('Star' can get a relatively high probability weight in the topic science for one definition, 
and get a relatively high probability weight in the topic 'sports' for a completely different definition of the word)

## Generative Model for Text Mining
*a probabilistic story that describes how the observed text could have been prodcued*
**The model pretends that the observed text was generated by a simple random process controlled by parameters A.**

1. Design a model for the data. There exists a small number of topics, each topic is a list of words w/ probabilities. 
Theta_j = the word-probability list for topic j
2. Every document is a mixture of those topics. (one doc could be mostly about this or that)
pi_i1, pi_i2, pi_ik = mixture precentages for document i
3. Two things to write a word in a document: 
- pick which topic to use right now (according to the document's mixture)
- pick a word from that topic's list of word probabilities
A = all of the thetas and Pis together. 
P(Data | Mode,A) = how likely is it that we would have seen exactly these documents if the topics and precentages were this particular A?
Note: Repeating step three for every word in every document, you get the whole collection.
